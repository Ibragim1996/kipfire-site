My Story: Why I Built KipFire
I live in Seattle. I'm an immigrant, and right now I work food delivery and construction jobs to support my life. KipFire started in 2024, but the story behind it started much earlier.
Trying many things that weren't mine
For years I tried a lot of different things. I'd start something, feel it wasn't for me, and move on. I thought about my own clothing brand with printed logos. I thought about a supplement brand. But every time, the same thought came back: this isn't mine.
And there was one problem behind almost everything: content.
My biggest struggle: the camera
For the last 6 or 7 years I watched other people create content. Some shared useful things, some showed their talent, some just showed who they are. I saw the internet as a huge chance to express yourself.
But I couldn't do it. I struggled to talk on camera. Like many people, I felt that making content was something silly, something "people like me" don't do. Moving to another country helped me break that barrier a little.
Then I hit the next wall. I'd start posting, and two weeks later I'd burn out. I saw perfect content from others and tried to make mine perfect too. I spent hours, sometimes a whole day, on one video: searching for ideas, filming, editing. It wasn't sustainable.
The idea: build something useful
Then an idea came to me: what if I built something that could actually help people with this problem?
I had no development skills. I still don't have a developer background. All I had was the goal to build. Using AI tools, I spent more time reading and learning than building at first. Development took about a year and a half.
Building from my car
I'm a family man, and we lived in a studio. There was no quiet place and no free time to work. About 80% of KipFire was built in my car, between food deliveries or after construction work, with a laptop on my lap. It was hard to focus. I had a strong goal, but also constant financial pressure and bills to pay. That pressure is still here today.
I also learned that building a startup is one thing, but growing it is even harder. I didn't know how startups work, how investors work, or how any of this system works. The only thing in my head was: keep building.
Doing it alone
The hardest part was not having someone next to me who wanted to reach for something big too. That hurt, and honestly it still does. But it taught me something important: if you really want something, you often have to walk the road yourself. It's hard to accept, but I believe it makes you stronger.
What KipFire is for
KipFire is a simple tool for one big problem. You open the app, pick a prompt (a question, a challenge, a "would you rather", a hot take) and react to it on camera. The prompt stays in the video. One minute later your video is ready.
I use it myself. What used to take me a whole day now takes a minute.
I believe millions of people want to start creating content but never try, or quit too early. I hope KipFire reaches them and helps them show who they are. The app is still growing, and many features are on the way. Right now my main goal is to find the people who really have this problem, especially people who have never filmed themselves before.
Why human content matters
Today we see more and more AI-generated content getting views. Honestly, that hurts. KipFire is built to encourage real human content: real faces, real reactions, real emotions. I believe and hope that in the future, real human content will be valued more than anything artificial.
If you've ever wanted to start, this is your sign. Start now, with your own face.
