How to post every day without burning out

Posting daily sounds impossible when every video feels like a project. The fix isn't more motivation. It's making each video smaller.

Make the video smaller

A daily video doesn't need a script, a location or editing. One prompt, one reaction, 15 to 30 seconds. If a video takes more than ten minutes from idea to post, it's too big to do every day.

Separate ideas from filming

Burnout usually comes from deciding what to post, not from filming. Collect ideas when you're not filming. In KipFire, tap Later to save cards you like, so when it's time to film the decision is already made.

Batch on one day

Film four or five short videos in one session while you're already set up. Post one a day. On busy days you have something ready.

Rotate formats

Doing the same format every day is what makes it feel like a chore. Rotate: a question on Monday, a would-you-rather on Tuesday, a hot take on Wednesday. Different formats use different energy, and your audience gets variety.

Count what you made, not what you got

Views are out of your control early on. The number of videos you made isn't. Track that. KipFire's Studio tab counts every reaction you save, which is a better early goal than likes.

Allow short days

Some days the video is ten seconds long. That still counts. Consistency beats quality at the start, because quality comes from doing it a lot.
