5 hooks for talking-head Reels that stop the scroll

On short video, people decide in about two seconds whether to keep watching. If you're talking to the camera, your first sentence is your hook. Here are five formats that work, even if you're new.

1. Start with the answer

Don't build up to your point. Say it first. Instead of "So today I want to talk about mornings," open with "I stopped using an alarm and I wake up earlier now." The claim makes people stay for the explanation.

2. Say the thing people disagree with

A hot take works as a hook because viewers stay to argue. "Breakfast food is better at dinner" gets more comments than "here's what I ate today." Keep it honest, not rude.

3. Ask a question they want to answer

"Would you rather lose your phone or your voice for a week?" Viewers answer in their heads, then in the comments. Show the question as text on screen so it lands even with the sound off.

4. Show the moment before the result

For challenges, open right before the attempt: "I have to hold eye contact for 30 seconds without laughing. Go." People stay to see if you make it.

5. Start mid-sentence

Cut the greeting and the intro. Start the clip halfway into a thought: "...and that's when I realized I'd been doing it wrong for ten years." It feels like the viewer walked into a conversation, and they want the beginning.
